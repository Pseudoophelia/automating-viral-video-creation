# Story rules

The director's taste, learned from her corrections across two series.

## Hook

- Strong hook in the first frame, with a hook line. The audience decides in
  the first second.
- Establish the antagonist's hostility early so the payoff lands.

## Teaser openings

- Opening on a charged moment from later in the episode, then returning to
  it, is standard short-drama practice. A repeated line or shot is a
  deliberate teaser. Never remove it as a "duplicate."
- The teaser and the payoff should be the same beat: set it up cold, pay it
  off warm.

## Twists

- Simple and genuinely weird, with a social statement. It must land in one
  sentence and reverse who carries the consequences.
- Reject metaphor-heavy or rule-heavy ideas. If the twist needs explaining,
  it is wrong.
- Steer back to the story's existing elements before inventing new ones.

## Reveals

- Keep secrets longer than feels comfortable. If the point is that no one can
  see the thing, the viewer never sees it either. The mystery is the tension.
- Give the audience a reason to care about the heroine before the reveal.

## Comedy (reality-show parody)

- The characters are young, ignorant and a little shallow. They do not really
  know what they are committing to. Play that up in the dialogue.
- Lean into insider knowledge of the world being parodied. Find one
  connective thread (an in-world hobby, food, event) and keep returning to
  it; it ties scenes together.
- End an episode by teasing something new (a secret, a reveal), not by
  resolving the current thread.

## Dialogue

- It must sound like the real show and the real world. Research how people on
  the source show actually talk, including interview/confessional segments.
- People do not talk in exposition. If no human would say the line, cut it.
- Each character keeps a distinct voice (for example: one dry and skeptical,
  one dumb and casual). Do not flatten them into the same register.
- Evolve the script slightly to fit the footage once clips exist; the script
  serves the cut, not the other way around.
