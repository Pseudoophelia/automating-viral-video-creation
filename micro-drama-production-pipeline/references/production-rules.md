# Production rules

How the work gets done around the documents.

## Research before building

- Before making shots, research basic filmmaking technique: shot sizes,
  camera moves, coverage, and editing basics.
- If the episode follows an existing show's format, research that show's real
  shot grammar (where interviews happen, how scenes are covered, how episodes
  are paced and edited). Build the storyboard on that grammar, not on
  guesses.
- Prefer practitioners' real workflows (what working creators actually do)
  over tool marketing.

## Lock before spend

- Lock the script, exact dialogue, characters and voices before generating
  anything. Prove one scene with its dialogue before funding a full episode.
- Credits are spent only with per-batch approval from the director. Free-plan
  credits are still spend: report the balance before and after.
- Within an approved batch, finish the batch and flag flaws rather than
  stopping mid-batch over one flawed clip.

## One platform, one job

- Do not try to do everything at once. When working with Midjourney, get the
  image right. When working with Veo, get the shot right. When working with
  ElevenLabs, get the voices and dialogue right. Mixing them in one pass is
  how mismatched cuts happen.

## After footage exists

- Watch the clips, then evolve the script slightly to fit them.
  Regenerate voices to match footage before ever regenerating footage to
  match voices; voice credits are cheaper.
- If the footage was built for an older script, do not patch: rebuild the
  storyboard from the current script, shot by shot, and get it approved
  before new clips are made.

## Division of labor

- The assistant carries script and dialogue from the storyboard, reference
  prep, prompts and continuity checks.
- The director directs, approves, controls spending, and does or approves the
  final edit.
